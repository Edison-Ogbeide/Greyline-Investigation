What You Receive

A hire-or-don't-hire intelligence report.

The deliverable is structured to support a hiring decision — not to bury HR or hiring managers in raw data. Each section flags findings clearly, with a risk-rated executive summary at the front and supporting evidence behind.

The Pre-Hire Intelligence Report

Standard reports are typically 12–18 pages; Executive reports are typically 25–40 pages. Both are delivered as encrypted PDFs via secure link, structured for clarity, with a clear executive summary and risk rating at the front.

Where findings are clean, the report confirms it plainly. Where concerns emerge — undisclosed prior employment, adverse media, regulatory action, hidden directorships — the report sets them out with evidence and a risk-weighted commentary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What employers need to know.

Does the candidate need to consent to this work?

Yes — for the elements that legally require it. Criminal record checks (DBS Basic, Standard, Enhanced) require the subject's consent and signature. Reference verification is conducted with the subject's awareness — that's the entire point of the references they've supplied. Employment history verification can typically be conducted with the subject's express authorisation as part of the offer process.

The digital footprint review and adverse media screening are conducted from open and lawful sources and do not require subject consent — they cover information the candidate has chosen to make publicly available about themselves.

Is this lawful in the UK?

Yes. Pre-Hire Intelligence operates under the UK Data Protection Act 2018 and GDPR (UK). Lawful basis is documented at the start of every engagement — typically the legitimate interests of the employer in confirming the suitability of a candidate. Where consent is legally required (criminal checks), it is obtained and documented before that element of the work begins.

How does the engagement begin?

The employer instructs the work via online checkout or via a written engagement for volume contracts. We send a candidate-facing intake link covering only what the candidate must complete (consents, document uploads, ID verification), so the employer doesn't have to manage that workflow. The candidate completes the intake; we conduct the remaining work in parallel and deliver to the employer.

What is volume pricing?

Employers commissioning 5 or more reports per month are eligible for volume contract pricing. This typically includes: discounted unit pricing, a dedicated account manager, an embedded candidate intake portal, and consolidated invoicing. Contact us for volume contracts before instructing your first report — discounts cannot be applied retroactively.

How long does the work take?

Standard tier: typically 5–10 working days from candidate intake completion. Executive tier: typically 10–15 working days, longer where source-country field verification is required (which can extend by 5–15 days depending on jurisdiction). Expedited delivery is available where business need requires; quoted at the point of instruction.

What if the candidate refuses to consent or fails to complete intake?

The report is delivered with a clear note of which components could not be completed and why. Where a candidate refuses consent for criminal record checks but completes other elements, the employer receives the partial report alongside guidance on what the absence of certain checks means in their hiring context. We do not charge full fee for substantially incomplete engagements; pricing is adjusted at delivery if material components could not be completed.

What if findings recommend not hiring?

The report sets out findings clearly and provides a risk-rated recommendation. The decision remains entirely with the employer. UK employment law requires that hiring decisions are made on lawful grounds — the report supports that decision-making, but does not replace the employer's own legal and HR judgement.

Where findings include matters that the employer is legally required to disregard (spent convictions under the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act, for example), the report flags them appropriately and the employer's HR or legal team can apply the relevant legal rules.

How does this differ from a standard background check provider?

Standard providers run automated checks against databases and produce templated outputs. Greyline is intelligence-led — every report is reviewed and contextualised by an analyst before delivery. The Tier 2 (Standard) and Tier 3 (Executive) digital footprint components are work that automated providers do not undertake. Source-country field verification on foreign hires is work that no standard provider offers in this price band.
