### The Fixed Structure

Every Greyline report follows the same structure, regardless of the capability it sits under. This consistency means a client, or their legal adviser, knows where to find what they need.

| Section | Content | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Executive Summary** | A non-technical overview of the question, the approach, and the headline findings. Written for decision-makers who will not read the full detail. | Actionability without an analyst in the room. |
| **2. Scope and Methodology** | The question we were asked to answer, the disciplines applied, the sources used, the limitations encountered, and the standard to which the work was conducted. | Transparency and reproducibility. |
| **3. Findings** | The corroborated findings, presented in logical order, each with source citation and reliability grading. | The evidential core of the report. |
| **4. Supporting Evidence** | Screenshots, documents, captures, and other material referenced in the findings. Organised and indexed for easy cross-reference. | Verifiability. |
| **5. Analyst Assessment** | Where appropriate, the analyst's reasoned judgement on what the findings mean, clearly separated from the findings themselves. | Context and inference, properly labelled. |
| **6. Appendices** | Unverified leads, supplementary material, glossary of terms, and technical detail that supports but does not form part of the main findings. | Completeness without confusion. |
| **7. Analyst Sign-Off** | Name, date, and statement of personal accountability. | Named responsibility. |

<hr class="gold-rule">

### Executive Summary: The Most-Read Section

The executive summary is written for the client who needs to act on the report without reading thirty pages of detail. It answers:

- What were you asked to find out?
- What did you find?
- What does it mean?
- What should the client do next?

It is deliberately non-technical. It does not replace the detailed findings; it translates them for decision-making.

<hr class="gold-rule">

### Scope and Methodology: The Audit Trail

This section is critical for litigation-aligned engagements and for any client who needs to defend the report's conclusions under scrutiny. It covers:

- **The precise question** as defined in the scoping note. If the question shifted during the engagement, this is documented.
- **The disciplines applied** and why they were selected.
- **The sources consulted** and any significant sources that were unavailable or uncooperative.
- **The limitations encountered**, jurisdictional gaps, private accounts, deleted material, source unavailability. We do not hide constraints.
- **The standard**, reference to the Greyline Verification Standard, ACPO Principles, or other applicable framework.

This section exists so that an independent third party can understand what was done, why, and what the boundaries were.

<hr class="gold-rule">

### Findings: The Evidential Core

Findings are presented in a standard format:

> **[Finding Number]**: [Statement of fact]<br>
> **Source**: [Source description, date, location]<br>
> **Reliability**: [Source reliability grade]<br>
> **Corroboration**: [Independent source confirming the same fact, where available]

This format makes it immediately clear what is known, how well it is known, and whether it stands alone or is supported.

<hr class="gold-rule">

### Supporting Evidence: Organised and Indexed

Evidence is not dumped at the back of the report. It is:

- **Numbered** to correspond with finding references
- **Described** so the reader knows what they are looking at
- **Sourced** with URL, timestamp, or collection method
- **Preserved** in native format where possible (for example, the original screenshot file, not just a printout)

For litigation-aligned engagements, evidence is provided in a separate, indexed bundle with chain-of-custody documentation.

<hr class="gold-rule">

### Analyst Assessment: Inference, Not Fact

Where the analyst offers a view on what the findings mean, it is clearly labelled as **Assessment** and includes:

- The reasoning that leads to the assessment
- The findings it is based on
- Any alternative interpretations considered and why they were rejected
- The analyst's confidence level in the assessment

This separation protects the client. They can rely on the findings as fact. They can evaluate the assessment as informed opinion.

<hr class="gold-rule">

### Litigation-Aligned Reporting

For engagements where the report may be used in legal proceedings, we provide:

- **CPR Part 35 compliant** formatting where instructed
- **Expert witness statement** preparation where required
- **Chain-of-custody documentation** as standard
- **Disclosure-ready** evidence bundles

The report is written with the assumption that it will be read by a hostile party and tested by cross-examination. That assumption raises the standard.

### What This Means for the Client

You receive a report that is:
- **Consistent**: The same structure every time, so you know where to look.
- **Transparent**: Scope, methodology, limitations, and sources are all disclosed.
- **Defensible**: Written to a standard that withstands legal scrutiny.
- **Actionable**: A non-technical summary tells you what to do next.
