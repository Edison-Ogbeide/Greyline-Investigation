Why these three components together

The Tier 3 footprint tells you who the person is online — including what they've concealed. Criminal and background checks tell you what authorities know about them. Identity verification confirms they are actually who they claim to be on paper. None of these three, individually, is sufficient for a decision as consequential as sponsorship or marriage. Together, they leave very little space for the kind of deception that would otherwise emerge years later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Things people need to know before instructing.

Will my partner — or prospective partner — find out about this report?

The Tier 3 digital intelligence component is conducted from open and lawful sources, leaving no trace with the subject. The criminal record checks may, depending on the country, require the subject's authorisation (UK DBS Standard or Enhanced, for example, cannot be obtained without the subject's consent and signature).

For some country-of-origin checks, authorisation can be obtained as part of a pre-marriage or pre-sponsorship process the subject already understands. For others, only checks that do not require authorisation are conducted. We confirm at the discovery / intake stage which checks can be lawfully completed in your specific case before commencing.

Is this lawful in the UK?

Yes. The engagement is conducted under the UK Data Protection Act 2018 and GDPR (UK) on confirmed lawful basis. Every component complies with the law of the jurisdiction it is performed in. We document lawful basis at the start of the engagement and decline any element that would require crossing lawful lines.

What if my partner is from a country with weak public records?

This is one of the most common reasons clients commission this report. In countries where public records are limited, fragmented, or unreliable, in-country verification by our trusted local agents fills the gap — confirming address, employer, family, marital history, and identity in person. Where formal records cannot be obtained, the dossier states what was attempted, what was found, and what could not be lawfully verified, so you have the complete picture of what is and isn't known.

What does "country-of-origin field verification" mean?

For some cases, particularly diaspora marriages or sponsorship contexts, the client wants confirmation in the subject's home country that the address they've stated is real, the employer they've claimed exists, the family they've described is who they say they are. Greyline coordinates trusted in-country agents to do this work. Where included, this is quoted separately because the cost varies significantly by country (£500 in some, £1,500+ in others).

What if you find something that recommends declining the commitment?

The dossier states findings clearly with the underlying evidence. We do not soften findings to spare the client; the value of the report depends on its honesty. The recommendation will be specific — not "decline" in the abstract but "we recommend not proceeding with sponsorship until X is resolved" or "we identified an undisclosed prior marriage in [country]; we recommend you discuss this directly with the subject before continuing."

The decision remains entirely yours. Greyline provides the intelligence; you make the decision.

Can the report support an immigration application?

The report is not designed to be submitted to UK Visas & Immigration. It is designed to support your private decision before you sponsor. Where the engagement uncovers documents or facts that the subject has misrepresented in their own immigration application, we recommend you consult an immigration solicitor before submitting any sponsorship.

Can solicitors instruct this report on behalf of clients?

Yes. We routinely accept instructions from family solicitors, immigration solicitors, and private client lawyers on behalf of their clients. Engagement letters can be issued to either the firm or the client directly, and we can work under instructions that preserve litigation privilege where relevant.

How does pricing work for non-UK subjects?

The £2,499 fee covers UK-based work and country-of-origin checks that can be performed remotely. Where country-of-origin field verification is required (in-country agents conducting physical checks), this is quoted separately at the discovery stage based on country, scope, and depth — typically between £500 and £2,000 in addition to the base fee.

What happens to the report after delivery?

You receive the encrypted PDF and may retain it for your own use. Greyline retains the working files and report under our Data Protection & Confidentiality Policy retention schedule, then securely destroys them. The report is not shared with third parties under any circumstances without your written consent.
