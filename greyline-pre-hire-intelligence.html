The question this answers

"They look great on paper. The references checked out. The DBS came back clean. So why does something feel off — and what am I missing?"

Standard background checks tell you what a candidate has not been formally convicted of. They tell you that the references they nominated will say complimentary things. They do not tell you who the person actually is. Most of the meaningful risk in a senior or trust-critical hire — fabricated employment history, undisclosed business interests, social media behaviour incompatible with the role, hidden disciplinary records, conduct on prior teams that quietly led to departure — sits entirely outside what compliance-tier screening surfaces. Greyline Pre-Hire Intelligence is built to close that gap.

The product is offered in two tiers. Standard (£499) sits above mid-market vetting providers in scope, covering identity and right-to-work verification, criminal record check, employment and education verification through documented sources, reference authentication, and a digital footprint and adverse media review. Executive (£1,749) sits below boutique investigative firms in price while delivering comparable depth: extended criminal checks, directorship and litigation searches, the full Digital Behaviour Intelligence Report, PEP and sanctions screening, and source-country field verification for foreign hires.

Built for

The roles where a bad hire costs more than the vetting ever could.

Pre-Hire Intelligence is designed for senior, trust-critical, and exposure-significant hires: C-suite, finance leadership, partner-level legal and accounting roles, household and personal staff for HNW families, regulated firm hires, and any role where the cost of a wrong hire — financial, reputational, or operational — runs into multiples of the cost of properly vetting the candidate.

For employers running active hiring programmes, volume contracts are available. Embedded candidate intake portals, dedicated account management, and discounted volume pricing are all on the table for organisations commissioning five or more reports per month.
