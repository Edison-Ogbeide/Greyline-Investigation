The question this answers

"Is the person I'm talking to who they say they are, and is this a romance scam?"

Romance fraud now ranks among the most financially damaging crimes targeting individual victims in the UK and the diaspora — Action Fraud reports victims losing five and six-figure sums to perpetrators who never existed in the form they presented. The Greyline Romance Fraud Investigation is a focused, decision-ready inquiry into a single individual you have met online or who has approached you online: a structured determination of whether the relationship pattern matches known romance fraud typologies, and whether the person you are speaking to is real, present, and who they claim to be.

This is not a Google search and it is not a database lookup. It is an analyst-led investigation built around the specific facts you give us: the photographs they have sent, the platforms you've spoken on, the claims they have made about themselves, and the financial or emotional asks they have made of you. We then test those claims against the public web, the dating ecosystem, and the structured signatures that fraud actors leave behind.

What is included

What we actually do.

  • Photo provenance and reverse image search. Every photograph the subject has provided is run through reverse image search, facial geometry comparison, and stock-image and stolen-content databases. We are looking for the same face appearing on other identities, the same images appearing on multiple dating profiles, and the visual signatures of AI-generated or manipulated media.
  • Identity claim verification. Every factual claim the subject has made — name, employment, location, marital status, citizenship, military or professional credentials — is tested against documented public sources. Where claims cannot be verified, we say so and explain why.
  • Cross-platform alias and account discovery. The subject's username, email, and phone number are searched across 300+ social, dating, professional, and forum platforms. Patterns of repeated profile creation, recently-created accounts, and inconsistent presentation across platforms are documented.
  • Romance-fraud typology assessment. The relationship pattern itself is mapped against the documented behavioural signatures of romance fraud: love-bombing pace, the introduction of a financial crisis, isolation tactics, refusal to video call or meet, claims of geographic distance, and the structured progression toward a financial ask.
  • Structured findings report. A PDF report (typically 12–18 pages) presenting confirmed findings, sources, screenshots, and a clear assessment: red flag, amber flag, or no significant indicators. Recommendations follow the assessment.
