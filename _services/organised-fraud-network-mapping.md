title: "Organised Fraud Network Mapping"
slug: organised-fraud-network-mapping
description: "Mapping the individuals, companies and infrastructure behind an organised fraud operation, beyond the single incident that first surfaced it."
problem_headline: "The fraud you caught is rarely the whole operation."
problem_statement: >-
  A single confirmed fraud incident, a fraudulent invoice, a scam
  targeting one employee, a fake supplier, is often the visible edge of
  a wider, organised operation running the same playbook against
  multiple targets simultaneously. Treating it as an isolated event
  means missing the linked companies, phone numbers, email
  infrastructure and cryptocurrency wallets that connect it to a
  broader network, and losing the chance to understand how likely you
  are to be targeted again.
deliverable: >-
  A structured network map of the individuals, companies and
  infrastructure connected to a fraud operation, built from a
  confirmed incident outward, identifying linked entities, shared
  infrastructure, and patterns consistent with an organised, repeat
  operation.
features:
  - "Known suspects and the companies connected to them"
  - "Shared infrastructure: phone numbers, email domains, and payment channels"
  - "Cryptocurrency wallets connected to the network"
  - "Patterns linking this incident to other reported fraud"
  - "A report suitable for law enforcement referral or internal risk assessment"
methodology: >-
  Network and link analysis combining dark web and open-source
  intelligence, corporate registry checks on connected entities, and
  blockchain analysis where cryptocurrency infrastructure is involved,
  with every finding verified by a named analyst before it is
  reported.
turnaround: "5 to 7 business days from confirmed scope."
faq:
  - q: "Do you need more than one incident to start this mapping?"
    a: "No. A single confirmed incident is often enough to begin, since the connected infrastructure, shared phone numbers, domains, or company names, frequently reveals the wider network from that starting point."
  - q: "Can this support a law enforcement report?"
    a: "Yes. Findings are documented to a standard suitable for a police or Action Fraud report, and can strengthen a report considerably by showing it is part of a wider, organised pattern."
  - q: "How does this differ from Fraud Network Monitoring?"
    a: "This is the initial mapping exercise, building the network from a confirmed incident. Fraud Network Monitoring is the continuous subscription that watches the mapped network for new activity afterward."
  - q: "What if the network turns out to be larger than expected?"
    a: "That is a common outcome, and scope is adjusted transparently with you as the picture develops, rather than silently expanding the engagement."
