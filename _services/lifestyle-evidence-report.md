description: "Documented evidence of a subject's visible lifestyle and spending, where it is inconsistent with their declared income or financial position."
problem_headline: "A lifestyle that does not match a declared income is a pattern, not proof, until it is documented properly."
problem_statement: >-
  Disputes involving financial disclosure, matrimonial finance
  proceedings, insurance claims where lifestyle contradicts a
  disability or injury claim, and disputes where a party's declared
  income does not match their visible spending all turn on the same
  question: can the gap between what someone says and how they
  actually live be documented to an evidential standard, or is it just
  an impression. An impression does not move a case forward. Sourced,
  dated evidence does.
deliverable: >-
  A structured lifestyle evidence report documenting a subject's
  visible spending, assets and activities against their declared
  financial position, sourced and dated to support use in proceedings
  or a formal dispute.
features:
  - "Property, vehicles and visible high-value assets"
  - "Travel patterns and lifestyle indicators inconsistent with declared income"
  - "Public social media and business activity contradicting a stated position"
  - "Sourced, dated findings suitable for disclosure or evidential use"
  - "A clear summary linking findings to the specific inconsistency in question"
methodology: >-
  Lawful open-source research, property and asset record checks, and
  systematic documentation of publicly visible activity, gathered and
  timestamped to a standard suitable for use in family finance
  proceedings, insurance disputes, or other formal proceedings, with
  every finding verified by a named analyst.
turnaround: "3 to 5 business days from confirmed scope."

  - q: "Is evidence gathered this way admissible?"

  - q: "Does this only look at social media?"
    a: "No. Social media is one source among several, alongside property records, corporate filings and other publicly available evidence of lifestyle and spending."
  - q: "How far back can this evidence go?"
    a: "As far as the publicly available record allows. Historical evidence is often as relevant as current activity, since it can establish a pattern rather than a single snapshot."
