title: "Synthetic Media Authenticity Report"
slug: synthetic-media-authenticity-report
description: "A structured assessment of whether an image, video, audio clip or suspicious call is consistent with AI generation, cloning or manipulation, before you rely on it for a decision."
problem_headline: "Is what you're looking at, or listening to, real?"
problem_statement: >-
  A photograph, video clip, voice recording or suspicious phone call has come
  into question, on a dating profile, in a dispute, attached to an email,
  arriving as an urgent request, and you need to know whether it holds up
  before you rely on it or act on it. Visual and aural inspection alone are
  no longer a reliable test. The artefacts that used to give synthetic media
  away are increasingly absent, short voice clips are now enough to produce
  a passable cloned voice, and a confident guess is not the same as a
  structured finding.
deliverable: >-
  A written report assessing the media in question, with findings worded as
  consistent with, or not consistent with, synthetic generation or
  manipulation. Every finding states what was tested, what was found, and how
  confident the assessment is, so you can see the basis for the conclusion
  rather than just the conclusion itself. Where the submission is a
  suspicious call, voicemail or voice message rather than a standalone clip,
  the report also cross-references the circumstances against documented
  vishing patterns as a matter of course, since a genuine voice can still be
  used in a scripted deception.
features:
  - "Provenance and metadata analysis of the file as submitted"
  - "Reverse image and video search across known stock, stolen and previously circulated content"
  - "Technical artefact analysis: compression inconsistencies, lighting and shadow analysis, facial and temporal consistency checks"
  - "Acoustic and technical analysis of audio, including comparison against genuine voice samples of the claimed speaker where available"
  - "For suspicious calls: cross-referencing of the circumstances against known vishing and social engineering typologies, urgency, an unusual financial or access request, refusal to verify by another channel"
  - "A confidence-graded finding for each item submitted, not a single yes or no verdict"
methodology: >-
  Technical detection tooling flags candidate indicators; every flag is then
  reviewed and verified by a named analyst before it enters the report.
  Detection tooling in this space is probabilistic, so findings are worded as
  consistent with, not proof of, synthetic generation, and the reasoning
  behind each finding is disclosed rather than asserted. Where a submission
  involves a call or voice message, technical audio analysis is combined
  with behavioural cross-referencing against known vishing scripts, reported
  as a distinct strand alongside the authenticity finding.
turnaround: "3 to 5 business days from receipt of the media in question, depending on volume. A litigation-grade tier is available for evidence in dispute, adding CPR Part 35 compliant formatting, a formal rebuttal statement, and expert witness availability, typically extending turnaround to 5 to 15 business days depending on complexity."
faq:
  - q: "Can you prove something is a deepfake?"
    a: "No, and any report that claims to is overstating what the underlying technology can support. Detection tooling is probabilistic, so we word findings as consistent with, or not consistent with, synthetic generation, with the reasoning disclosed."
  - q: "What kinds of media can you assess?"
    a: "Still images, video, and audio, including suspicious calls and voice messages. Where a matter involves more than one media type, for example a profile photo and a subsequent video call, we assess them together as part of the same picture."
  - q: "What if there is no recording of a suspicious call?"
    a: "We can still assess the circumstances against known vishing patterns, though the technical voice analysis strand requires an actual recording. If a call is ongoing or expected to recur, we advise on how to capture one lawfully. In the meantime, do not act on the request through the same channel it arrived on; verify independently using a known number or separate agreed method."
  - q: "Can this be used in a dispute or legal matter?"
    a: "Yes. Where required, request the litigation-grade tier: the report is prepared to CPR Part 35 standard, with chain-of-custody documentation, cross-referencing against the case timeline, and the analyst available to provide an expert witness statement or a formal rebuttal statement challenging evidence submitted by another party."
  - q: "What if the evidence turns out to be genuine?"
    a: "That is reported as clearly as a finding of fabrication would be. Our obligation is a defensible finding, not a result that favours whoever instructed us."
