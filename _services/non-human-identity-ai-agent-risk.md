title: "Non-Human Identity & AI Agent Risk Assessment"
slug: non-human-identity-ai-agent-risk
description: "Assessing exposure to AI agents and automated identities operating within your systems, customer-facing channels, or counter-party interactions."
problem_headline: "Not everyone, and not everything, you are dealing with is human."
problem_statement: >-
  Automated agents and bots, some sanctioned, some not, increasingly operate
  inside business systems and customer-facing channels: a chatbot with more
  access than intended, an unauthorised AI agent connected to internal tools,
  or a counter-party interaction that is not actually being conducted by a
  person. Left unassessed, this is a growing and often invisible category of
  risk.
deliverable: >-
  An assessment identifying non-human and AI-agent activity within a defined
  scope, whether that is customer-facing channels, internal systems, or a
  specific counter-party relationship, mapping what access or influence that
  activity has, and setting out the exposure it represents.
features:
  - "Behavioural pattern analysis to distinguish human from automated or AI-driven activity"
  - "Technical review of agent and bot access, permissions, and integration points where in scope"
  - "Cross-referencing against documented patterns of AI-agent abuse and impersonation"
  - "A clear statement of what was in scope and what was not, so the assessment's boundaries are explicit"
  - "Prioritised recommendations addressing the highest-exposure findings first"
methodology: >-
  Behavioural analysis combined with technical review of the systems and
  channels in scope. As elsewhere in this capability, findings on whether a
  given actor is AI-driven are worded as consistent with, not proof of,
  automation.
turnaround: "5 to 10 business days, depending on the number of systems and channels in scope."
faq:
  - q: "Is this a technical audit or an intelligence assessment?"
    a: "Both, in combination. Where deep technical system access is required beyond what this assessment covers, we scope that separately with our Cyber & Digital Risk Intelligence capability."
  - q: "Can this cover a specific counter-party we suspect is not who they claim to be?"
    a: "Yes. This is a common trigger, a supplier or counter-party interaction that behaves inconsistently with genuine human correspondence, and the assessment can be scoped narrowly around that relationship."
  - q: "What happens if you find an unauthorised AI agent in our systems?"
    a: "We document what it had access to and for how long, so you and your technical team can assess impact and remediate, prioritised by exposure rather than delivered as an undifferentiated list."
