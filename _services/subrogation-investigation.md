title: "Subrogation Investigation"
slug: subrogation-investigation
description: "Intelligence to support an insurer's subrogated recovery claim, identifying the liable third party and their capacity to pay."
problem_headline: "Establishing liability is only half of a subrogation claim. Establishing capacity to pay is the other."
problem_statement: >-
  Insurers pursuing a subrogated recovery against a liable third party
  routinely have the liability question well covered by their legal
  and claims teams. What is covered less consistently is whether the
  third party actually has the assets or insurance to satisfy a
  recovery claim, and whether their corporate structure has been
  correctly identified, before legal costs are committed to pursuing
  it. A recovery claim against the wrong entity, or an entity with
  nothing to recover from, is money spent for no return.
deliverable: >-
  An intelligence report on a liable third party in a subrogation
  matter, verifying corporate structure, known assets, insurance
  status where identifiable, and financial standing, to inform the
  decision to pursue recovery and the strategy for doing so.
features:
  - "Correct identification of the liable legal entity"
  - "Known assets and financial standing relevant to recovery prospects"
  - "Insurance status, where publicly or contractually identifiable"
  - "Prior litigation and settlement history for the same entity"
  - "A clear recommendation on whether pursuit is commercially worthwhile"
methodology: >-
  Corporate registry and property record analysis, court record
  searches for prior litigation and settlement patterns, and financial
  standing research, delivered in a format built to inform a recovery
  strategy decision, with every finding verified by a named analyst.
turnaround: "3 to 5 business days from confirmed scope."
faq:
  - q: "Do you work directly with insurers' in-house recovery teams?"
    a: "Yes, this is a core part of our Fraud & Asset Intelligence work, and we are used to coordinating directly with claims handlers and recovery teams on their existing casework and timelines."
  - q: "What if the third party has no meaningful assets?"
    a: "That is reported clearly, so a decision on whether to pursue recovery can be made on the actual economics of the claim rather than assumption."
  - q: "Can this identify whether the third party has relevant insurance?"
    a: "Where insurance status is identifiable through public or contractual sources, this is included, since it materially changes recovery prospects and strategy."
  - q: "How does this differ from a standard credit check on the third party?"
    a: "A credit check gives a financial snapshot. This investigation also verifies corporate structure, prior litigation behaviour, and asset position, which together give a fuller picture of recovery viability."
