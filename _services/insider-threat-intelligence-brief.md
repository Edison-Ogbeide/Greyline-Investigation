---
title: "Insider Threat Intelligence Brief"
slug: insider-threat-intelligence-brief
description: "A focused intelligence assessment of insider risk connected to a specific employee, role, or department, before it becomes a confirmed incident."
problem_headline: "By the time insider risk is confirmed, the damage is usually already done."
problem_statement: >-
  Insider threat rarely announces itself. It surfaces as a pattern:
  unusual data access, a resignation that follows a competitor's job
  offer, a contractor with access beyond their role, or a tip that
  something is not right. Security teams often face a choice between
  ignoring the signal or launching a full internal investigation on
  thin evidence, either of which carries risk if handled wrong.
deliverable: >-
  A focused intelligence assessment of a named individual, role or
  department where insider risk is suspected, combining lawful digital
  footprint analysis, public record checks, and pattern assessment
  against known insider threat indicators, to inform whether and how to
  proceed.
features:
  - "Digital footprint and public activity review for indicators of risk"
  - "Assessment against recognised insider threat behavioural indicators"
  - "Corporate and financial interest checks for undisclosed conflicts"
  - "A clear recommendation on next steps, proportionate to the finding"
  - "An optional follow-up monitoring period where a concern is stood down but not entirely closed"
methodology: >-
  Lawful open-source and public record research combined with a
  structured insider threat indicator framework, conducted discreetly
  to avoid tipping off the subject before your organisation has decided
  how to proceed, with every finding verified by a named analyst.
turnaround: "48 to 72 hours from confirmed scope, given the time-sensitive nature of insider risk concerns."
faq:
  - q: "Will the individual know they are being assessed?"
    a: "No. This work is conducted discreetly using lawfully obtained information, without contacting the individual or alerting colleagues, so your organisation retains full control over what happens next."
  - q: "What if the assessment finds nothing concerning?"
    a: "That is a genuinely useful outcome. A clean finding lets you stand down a concern with confidence rather than carrying unresolved suspicion, and is reported as a clean finding, not inflated to justify the engagement."
  - q: "Can this lead to a formal workplace investigation?"
    a: "Yes, where findings warrant it. This brief is deliberately proportionate and often the step before a full Workplace Investigation is commissioned, once there is a documented basis for one."
  - q: "What kinds of signals typically trigger this brief?"
    a: "Unusual data access patterns, a resignation shortly before or after a competitor approach, a tip from a colleague, or access privileges that no longer match a departing employee's role are the most common triggers."
  - q: "What if the concern is lower-level but you want to keep watching?"
    a: "Where a finding does not warrant escalation but the underlying concern has not fully resolved, we can agree a follow-up monitoring period on the same named individual, publicly available information only, rather than instructing a fresh brief each time something changes."
