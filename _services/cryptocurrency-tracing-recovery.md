problem_statement: >-
  A common misconception treats cryptocurrency as anonymous and
  therefore unrecoverable once stolen or sent to a scammer. In reality,
  most blockchains are public ledgers, and transactions can be traced
  from wallet to wallet with the right analytical tools. What breaks
  the trail is not the technology, it is the absence of a
  professionally run tracing exercise before the funds reach a point
  where recovery is no longer practically possible, most commonly a
  regulated exchange that can be compelled to freeze and disclose.
deliverable: >-
  A blockchain tracing report following the movement of stolen or
  fraudulently obtained cryptocurrency from the point of loss, through
  any intermediate wallets or mixing services, to the point where
  funds reach a regulated exchange or other actionable destination.
features:
  - "Wallet-to-wallet transaction tracing across major blockchains"
  - "Identification of exchange deposits where funds become potentially recoverable"
  - "Detection of mixing services and layering techniques used to obscure the trail"
  - "Sanctioned address screening relevant to the destination wallets"
  - "A report structured to support a freezing request or law enforcement referral"
methodology: >-
  Blockchain analysis using transaction graph tooling combined with
  open-source intelligence on associated wallets, exchanges and known
  fraud infrastructure, with every finding verified by a named analyst
  before it is reported, and the evidential chain documented
  throughout.
turnaround: "48 to 72 hours from confirmed scope for an initial trace, with continuous monitoring available where funds are still moving."
faq:
  - q: "Is stolen cryptocurrency actually recoverable?"
    a: "It depends on where the funds end up. If they reach a regulated exchange, recovery is genuinely possible through a freezing request. If they remain in unhosted wallets indefinitely, recovery is harder, and we report that distinction honestly."
  - q: "How quickly do you need to be instructed after the loss?"
    a: "As soon as possible. Funds that reach an exchange and are withdrawn again before action is taken are much harder to recover, so speed matters more here than in most other investigations."
  - q: "Can this trace funds sent through a mixing service?"
    a: "Mixing services complicate but do not always defeat tracing. Findings are reported honestly on confidence level, what was established, what remains uncertain, rather than overstating what a mixer has actually obscured."
  - q: "Can this continue after the initial trace?"
    a: "Yes, through our Blockchain Intelligence Monitoring subscription, which continues watching the wallets involved for further movement."
