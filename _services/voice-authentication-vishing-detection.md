title: "Voice Authentication & Vishing Detection"
slug: voice-authentication-vishing-detection
description: "Assessing whether a suspicious call, voicemail or voice message is consistent with a cloned voice, and whether the approach matches known vishing patterns."
problem_headline: "The voice sounded right. That is no longer enough."
problem_statement: >-
  A call, voicemail, or voice message purporting to be from a family member,
  colleague, or executive has arrived with an urgent ask, a wire transfer, a
  password, an authorisation, and something about the situation feels
  engineered. Short voice clips are now sufficient to generate a passable
  synthetic voice, which means the old test of "I would know their voice"
  is no longer a reliable safeguard on its own.
deliverable: >-
  A structured assessment covering two things: whether the voice recording is
  consistent with synthetic generation or cloning, and whether the
  circumstances of the call match documented vishing patterns, urgency,
  an unusual financial or access request, refusal or inability to verify by
  another channel. The two strands are reported separately because a genuine
  voice can still be used in a scripted deception.
features:
  - "Acoustic and technical analysis of the recording, where one exists"
  - "Comparison against genuine voice samples of the claimed speaker, where available"
  - "Cross-referencing of the call's circumstances against known vishing and social engineering typologies"
  - "A confidence-graded finding on voice authenticity, not a binary verdict"
  - "Practical recommendations for verifying the caller independently before acting further"
methodology: >-
  Technical audio analysis combined with behavioural cross-referencing
  against known vishing scripts. As with all findings in this capability,
  voice authenticity is reported as consistent with, or not consistent with,
  cloning, never as definitive proof either way.
turnaround: "2 to 4 business days from receipt of the recording or a full account of the call."
faq:
  - q: "What if there is no recording of the call?"
    a: "We can still assess the circumstances against known vishing patterns, though the technical voice analysis strand requires an actual recording. If a call is ongoing or expected to recur, we advise on how to capture one lawfully."
  - q: "Is this useful for a single suspicious call, or only for a pattern of calls?"
    a: "Either. A single high-stakes call, for example one requesting an urgent wire transfer, is a common trigger for this service on its own."
  - q: "What should I do before the report is ready?"
    a: "Do not act on the request through the same channel the call arrived on. Verify independently, using a known number or a separate agreed method, before any funds move or access is granted."
