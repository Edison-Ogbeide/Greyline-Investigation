WHY WE EXIST

Most intelligence work happens
behind a brand, not a person.

The private investigation industry has a credibility problem, and most of it is self-inflicted. Firms hide behind generic logos and stock photos. Reports are written to be impressive rather than defensible. Clients pay retainers and receive theatre. When something goes wrong — a finding doesn't hold up in court, a methodology can't be explained — there is no one accountable, because there was never anyone named.

Greyline was built against that pattern. We are a small firm by choice. The work is led personally. Every report carries the name of the investigator who produced it. Methodology is documented at scoping stage and revisited at delivery. We do not take cases we cannot defensibly close — and we tell clients early when their case is not one of them.

OUR PRINCIPLES

The standards that govern
every engagement we accept.

Six principles. Three regulatory, three operational. They are not aspirational — they are the basis on which the firm decides what it will and will not do.

Regulatory · 01

UK GDPR & Data Protection Act 2018

All personal data we collect, hold, or process is governed by UK GDPR and the DPA 2018. We process only what is necessary, hold it only as long as required, and delete it on a documented schedule.

Regulatory · 02

ACPO Principles for Digital Evidence

Digital forensic work is conducted to the four ACPO Principles: original data is not altered; contemporaneous notes are kept; the process is reproducible by an independent third party; and the investigator in charge is personally accountable.

Regulatory · 03

Equality Act 2010 — Filtered Output

For pre-employment and screening engagements, output sent to the instructing employer is filtered to remove protected-characteristic information that is not role-relevant.

Operational · 01

Defensible-Closure Test

We do not accept cases we cannot close to a defensible standard. If the methodology required would be speculative or the data unobtainable lawfully, we tell the client at scoping stage — and decline.

Operational · 02

Email-First Communication

All client communication is by email and scheduled video call. We do not use WhatsApp or SMS for case correspondence — written communication is part of the audit trail.

Operational · 03

Service Boundary Doctrine

Where a service intersects with a regulated process — immigration, criminal proceedings, or regulatory enforcement — we publish the boundary explicitly on the service page.

WHAT WE WILL NOT DO

The work we refuse
defines the work we accept.

Every firm has a list of things it will not do. Most do not publish it. Ours is published because the boundary is the protection — for the client, the subject, and the firm.

  • We do not hack accounts or intercept private communications. All work is conducted on publicly available information, lawfully obtained records, or material disclosed with the informed consent of the subject.
  • We do not impersonate individuals to extract information. Pretexting, social engineering of subjects, and false-identity outreach are not part of our methodology and are not available on instruction.
  • We do not pay for unlawfully obtained data. Stolen data, breach dumps acquired from threat actors, and material sourced from corrupt insiders are excluded categorically — including where a client is willing to pay separately for them.
  • We do not accept enforcement-side immigration instruction. Our pre-visa relationship integrity work is commissioned by the sponsor only. We will not accept instruction from immigration authorities or sponsors seeking to substantiate the withdrawal of an existing sponsorship.
  • We do not inflate findings to justify a fee. A clean finding is reported as a clean finding. Fees are based on the work conducted, not on a particular outcome being produced.
  • We do not take work that requires us to mislead a court, a regulator, or a tribunal. Reports prepared for litigation are prepared to the standard the proceedings require — not the standard the instructing party would prefer.
  • We do not discuss live or historical cases publicly. Greyline does not produce promotional case studies that identify clients, and does not confirm or deny client relationships to third parties.
